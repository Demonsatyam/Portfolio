# City Pulse — cinematic explainer

**Know Before You Go.** 1920×1080, 30 fps, 2:05. Remotion project; every frame is code, every scene is its own editable composition.

## 1. Concept

One commuter, one morning, one incident. The film follows two journeys that meet:

- **The commuter** (teal jacket, white helmet, blue scooter, phone in a handlebar mount) leaves home on their usual route.
- **City Pulse** sees the accident first, through posts, a photo, a video and a form report, checks it, predicts where it will spread, and tells the commuter *before* they reach it. It also tells the nearest traffic control station, where an officer decides what to do.

One city model carries the whole film: the same streets, the same home (amber house), office (blue tower), incident (Ring Road flyover) and control station appear in every map shot. The camera moves between three worlds: **the city** (isometric, morning light), **the street** (side view, parallax skyline) and **the intelligence layer** (the same roads turned into a network).

Colour always means the same thing: **blue/cyan** = information and AI, **amber** = potential incident, **red** = verified danger or severe congestion, **green** = recommended route.

## 4. Asset design

- **Commuter**: one SVG character (`src/cast/Commuter.tsx`) in standing and riding poses. Teal jacket, white helmet with a cyan stripe, grey backpack, blue scooter, and a phone that rides in a handlebar mount. They never handle the phone while moving: the alert is read stopped at a red light.
- **Traffic officer**: white shirt, khaki trousers, peaked cap with a blue band, seated at the console (`src/cast/Officer.tsx`).
- **City**: one procedural isometric city (`src/city/`) used for every map shot, with fixed landmarks (home, office tower, control station, flyover incident). It can tilt between isometric and top-down and turn into a data network.
- **Agents**: abstract modules, never robots. Each is a rounded core with its own glyph and an orbit of activity (`src/cast/Agents.tsx`).
- **Phone UI, posts, report card**: `src/cast/UI.tsx`. Social posts and photos are drawn illustrations: demo content, not real people or real posts.
- **Type**: Inter Tight (display) and Inter (UI).

## 5. Accuracy guardrails followed

- Every report is checked before anyone is alerted; the Lake Road report is shown being stopped.
- Delays and the incident ID are labelled as simulated or demo data. No real travel-time savings are claimed.
- The officer decides and coordinates the response. City Pulse does not dispatch anyone or control signals ("City Pulse informs. People decide.").
- The order on screen is always: potential incident → verified incident → predicted impact → relevant user notification → human authority response.

## 6. Audio

- **Voice**: macOS on-device voice *Aman* (en-IN), one file per scene in `public/vo/`, regenerated from `scripts/narration.txt`.
- **Music and SFX**: synthesised by `scripts/music.mjs` and timed to the scene boundaries: piano and pads, then a low pulse for the problem, a 96 BPM arpeggio for the AI scenes, a riser into the alert, a major resolution and a final chord. The music ducks under the voice.

## 7. Editing and re-rendering

```bash
npm i
npx remotion studio                       # preview; each scene is also its own composition
node scripts/voice.mjs Aman 185           # re-voice after editing scripts/narration.txt (re-times every scene)
node scripts/music.mjs                    # re-score to the new timing
npx remotion render CityPulse out/city-pulse.mp4
```

To use a studio voice-over, drop `s01.wav`…`s11.wav` into `public/vo/` and run `node scripts/voice.mjs --skip-say`; scene lengths re-fit to the new takes.
